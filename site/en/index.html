Refuting the trace-distance case of axiom (T4) of Denis, Lacaille, Martin and Serrano-Ensástiga

Haobo Ma, Wenlin Zhang

Paper

Can a quantum spin become less directionally neutral under a process that treats every spatial direction equally? Haobo Ma and Wenlin Zhang show that, for one proposed measure of this neutrality, the answer is yes. Their counterexample overturns a proposed universal rule for mixed states, not the general possibility of measuring directional neutrality.

A system with fixed angular momentum, called a spin, can be represented using identical two-level systems, or qubits, restricted to states unchanged by permutations. A spin of size j corresponds to N = 2j qubits. Keeping t qubits and discarding the rest gives a reduced state. The proposed order-t anticoherence measure describes how close this reduction is to the uniform state on its symmetric space, whose dimension is t + 1. It equals one minus a normalized trace distance, so larger values mean greater anticoherence. For diagonal states, the unhalved trace distance is the sum of absolute differences between probabilities.

Denis, Lacaille, Martin and Serrano-Ensástiga proposed that this quantity should never decrease under a valid quantum channel commuting with rotations: rotating before processing should be equivalent to rotating afterward. They reported supporting numerical evidence but left a proof open. Ma and Zhang address the full mixed-state definition, which permits retaining up to all N qubits, rather than imposing the usual pure-state restriction to at most half. Their example uses spin four, represented by eight qubits, and retains seven. The channel acts on the nine-dimensional spin space; this representation does not mean that eight separate qubits undergo independent noise.

The authors build their channel from quadratic combinations of angular-momentum operators with their scalar part removed, called quadrupole operators. Summing the corresponding operator actions guarantees complete positivity, meaning compatibility with an auxiliary quantum system. An angular-momentum identity supplies the normalization that preserves total probability. Under rotation, the quadrupole operators mix like components of a Cartesian tensor; orthogonality cancels the rotation coefficients in the sum. This proves covariance under every rotation, rather than a finite sample. Their chosen mixed state is diagonal in the spin basis, and the channel preserves this diagonality, allowing exact probability calculations.

The seven-qubit input reduction has probabilities (21, 21, 40, 21, 21, 21, 21, 2)/168. Uniformity would give 21/168 everywhere: six entries agree, while two differ by opposite amounts of 19/168. The unhalved trace distance is therefore 19/84. Discarding one qubit from a symmetric component containing r ones leaves either r or r − 1 ones, with probabilities obtained by counting which bit was discarded. Applying this rule to the input and channel output gives an output distance of 3933/17248, slightly larger. After normalization, anticoherence changes by exactly −95/90552. This strictly negative rational number refutes the universal inequality.

The distinction is between general rotation-covariant channels and random mixtures of rotations. For mixtures of rotations, convexity and rotation invariance of trace distance force the reduced distance to decrease; the witness therefore lies outside that subclass. Symmetry of a global channel does not itself provide a distance-contracting channel acting on the retained subsystem alone. At full order, however, the authors prove monotonicity: the spin space has no smaller rotation-invariant sector, so covariance and probability preservation fix its uniform state. Ordinary trace-norm contraction then applies. The failure concerns partial reduction, not a breakdown of that contraction principle.

For this same witness, the trace-based measure increases at every other allowed order, and a separate fidelity-based candidate increases at all eight orders. The authors describe finite exact arithmetic certificates, using rational square-root bounds for fidelity rather than floating-point sign tests; these checks establish no universal rule. They credit the witness to a pinned upstream Lean declaration stating the mixed-state failure, while presenting their tensor proof and sibling comparisons separately as written arguments and exact calculations. This is a reconstruction, not a different counterexample. It also removes a hypothesis needed to apply an earlier conditional convex-roof construction generally to this measure, without refuting that conditional theorem or independently disproving quantum-side axioms. Universal fidelity monotonicity, trace monotonicity for mixed states retaining at most half the qubits, pure-input cases, and minimum violating spin and order remain unsettled here. Earlier proved purity and Hilbert–Schmidt constructions are unaffected.
